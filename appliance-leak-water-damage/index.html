Appliance leak emergency: first steps

A leaking appliance sits next to power and water, so safety comes first.

Do this first, in this order

  1. Close the shutoff valve behind or beside the appliance.
  2. Unplug the appliance only if the cord and outlet are dry and you are not standing in water.
  3. If the outlet or cord is wet, cut power at the breaker if you can reach it safely, otherwise call your electric utility.
  4. Pull the appliance forward only if the floor is not slick and you can do so without tipping it.
  5. Call (516) 845-9582 and tell us which appliance and which floor.

Do not run the appliance again until a technician has checked the hose or valve.

What to do before help arrives

Cut the water supply before anything else.

  • Close the shutoff valve behind the appliance or the main valve.
  • Unplug the appliance only if the cord and outlet are dry.
  • Do not open a washer or dishwasher door to let water run out across the floor.
  • Lift rugs and kitchen mats, and move small items up.
  • Photograph the appliance label and the wet floor.

Common appliance failures

Washing machines are the biggest source. Rubber hoses harden over time and braided supply hoses can burst at the crimp. Dishwashers leak at door seals or supply lines, and refrigerators leak from ice-maker lines and drain pans.

Water from these sources is usually clean or gray, but the dwell time matters. A leak that ran for hours soaks subfloors and adjoining rooms, and one that ran for days may have entered the wall behind.

Standards behind appliance leak cleanup

Cabinet bases and floors under a leaking washer or dishwasher are dried to the goals in the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard. A company that follows it should be able to show you readings, not just fans left running.

Under the NYC Department of Buildings rules, ordinary plumbing and relocation work must be done by a licensed master plumber. Hose replacement on a washing machine is a homeowner task, but a new supply line in the wall is not.

If you rent in a multi-unit building in the area, tell the landlord or superintendent the same day. Our Cambria Heights and Rosedale crews work with property managers and document the loss for the owner.

Preventing the next appliance leak

Most appliance leaks are preventable with a few minutes of attention a year. Replace rubber washer hoses with braided stainless lines, and check them for bulges or rust at the couplings. Pull the washer and dishwasher forward occasionally to check the floor and wall behind them for stains.

Shutoff valves seize if never used. Turn them to confirm they work, and replace one that sticks. If you travel often, shutting off the washer supply valves between uses limits what a failed hose can release.

Warning signs of a slow appliance leak

Slow leaks give clues before the floor fails.

Musty smell near the appliance

Often means water is collecting under flooring or in a cabinet base.

Lifting floor edges

Laminate and vinyl seams lift when moisture sits underneath.

Higher water bill

A running or dripping line can raise the bill without visible water.

Damp wall behind the unit

Supply lines inside the wall can weep before they fail.

If you notice any of these, shut off the supply and call a plumber and us.
