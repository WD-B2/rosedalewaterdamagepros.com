Flood exposure in Lawrence: what the Village says

The Village of Lawrence flood protection page addresses residents near Bannister Creek, Reynolds Channel and Brosewere Bay, and notes that many live in or near the 100 year floodplain, an area statistically expected to flood once every 100 years, with smaller floods more frequent. Flood maps are kept at the Peninsula Public Library and the Village Building Department.

The Building Department can tell you whether your property is in a floodplain, share flood depth and past flood information and arrange a property flood-hazard visit. Check your property before the next storm, not during it.

When water is spreading in Lawrence

If water is rising or spreading, safety comes before cleanup.

Do this first, in this order

  1. If a flood is imminent or you are advised to evacuate, leave for higher ground.
  2. If you have time and it is safe, shut off gas and electricity and move valuables to higher floors, as the Village advises.
  3. Stay out of floodwater, and keep children away from ditches and storm drains.
  4. Do not enter a flooded basement if water is near the panel. Call your electric utility or 911.
  5. When it is safe, call (516) 845-9582 and describe the depth and the floors affected.

Insurance, permits and licensed trades in Lawrence

The Village states that a homeowner's policy does not cover flood losses, that it participates in the National Flood Insurance Program, and that a flood policy has a 30-day waiting period before it takes effect. If you do not have flood coverage, ask an agent now, because it will not help after a flood has started.

The Village Building Department says a permit must be obtained for all construction including repairs, renovations, additions and demolition, and it licenses plumbers and electricians who work in the Village. Call or visit Village Hall, 196 Central Avenue, before starting repair work, and ask your contractor to confirm their Village license.

Sewer backup protection and what Lawrence advises

For sewer backup, the Village says a plug or standpipe can help if water is no more than one to two feet deep, and that for severe flooding a licensed plumber can install a backflow valve. Plugs are sold at plumbing supply warehouses, and a plumber installs standpipes.

If a backup has already happened, do not run water or flush. Our sewage backup cleanup page explains the safety steps, and the same advice applies to nearby Cambria Heights in Queens, where the city's 311 and DEP process applies instead.
