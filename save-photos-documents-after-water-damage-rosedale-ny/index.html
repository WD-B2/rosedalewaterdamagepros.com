How to Save Photos and Important Documents After Water Damage in Rosedale, NY

Paper and photos can often be saved if you act in the first day or two. Here is what to do in order.

  • Written for Rosedale homeowners
  • 24/7 dispatch
24/7 emergency dispatch(516) 845-9582☎ Call now

Wet photos, important papers and keepsakes can often be saved if you act quickly and gently. The first 48 hours matter most, because wet paper starts to stick, bleed and grow mold in that window.

This guide is for Rosedale, NY homeowners after a leak, flood or backup. If the water that soaked your belongings may have been contaminated, protect yourself first, and for the building itself, see flood damage cleanup and basement water damage cleanup.

Do this first, in the first hours

Safety first, then speed.

Do this first, in this order

  1. Make sure the area is safe to enter and wear gloves if the water was dirty.
  2. Move wet items to a clean, dry area and keep them from drying into a stack.
  3. Do not pull apart stuck photos or pages. Separate only if they come apart easily.
  4. Rinse mud off photos gently with clean water if needed.
  5. Dry items flat on clean paper or screens away from direct sun and heat.

If you cannot deal with them within a day or two, freezing them stops damage until you can.

Photographs

Photos are the most recoverable. Rinse silt gently, then lay them face up on clean paper towels, screens or a clean tabletop. Keep photos that are stuck together in a bowl of clean water to soak apart, and do not peel them.

Digital copies are often better than the originals after damage, so scan or photograph recovered images once they are dry.

Guidance on drying paper and photos

Public guidance from the EPA on wet materials points to a 24 to 48 hour window, and conservators give similar advice for paper and photographs. Acting inside it gives your items the best odds.

Documents you must replace and records you should photograph

Important papers such as titles, birth certificates and passports are replaced through the issuing office, and a copy of the damaged original helps. Take photos of what survived and what did not, because the DFS notes that you may need a detailed inventory with receipts, credit card statements and other proof of value for a claim.

Keep damaged property until the claim is settled, and ask your insurer before discarding items worth claiming.

Freezing as a pause button

If you cannot dry paper items within a day or two, freezing buys time. Place items in freezer bags, separating photos with wax paper if you can, and put them in a freezer. Cold stops mold growth and keeps pages from sticking further while you arrange professional help.

Freeze flat if possible. Do not use frost free settings for long storage if you can avoid it, since the cycle can dry items unevenly. Label bags so you can find things later.

Keepsakes and fabric

Textiles such as quilts, clothing and upholstery can often be laundered or cleaned if the water was clean. Rinse them promptly, avoid hot dryers on delicate items and let them dry fully before storing. If the water was contaminated, discard items that cannot be disinfected.

Furniture with sentimental value may be worth restoring. Ask a professional whether wood pieces can be dried slowly to avoid warping.

Books, files and boxes

Books swell and stick together, so stand them up with pages fanned open and let air move around them. Interleave sheets of clean paper in files that cannot be separated, and change the paper as it gets damp. Cardboard boxes weaken when wet, so move contents to plastic bins right away.

Working with a restoration company on contents

Pack-out means moving belongings to a clean, dry place for cleaning, drying and storage while the building is repaired. Ask what is included, how items are listed and how long they will be kept. A written inventory protects both sides and supports an insurance claim.

Key points to remember

Act in this order.

  • Make the area safe.
  • Move items to a clean, dry area.
  • Rinse mud gently from photos.
  • Air dry flat or freeze within 48 hours.
  • Copy important papers and replace originals through the issuer.
  • Keep damaged property until the claim is settled.

Notes for Rosedale

If the water that soaked your belongings was floodwater or sewage, the NYC Department of Environmental Protection says to thoroughly clean and disinfect inside your home, since floodwater may contain sewage and germs. Treat paper that sat in that water as contaminated and wear gloves when handling it.

Common questions

Can wet photos be saved?

Often yes, if you rinse and dry them within a day or two or freeze them.

Should I use a hair dryer on wet papers?

No. Heat can warp paper and set stains. Use air drying or freezing.

Is it safe to keep paper that was soaked by sewage?

Treat it as contaminated. DEP says to clean and disinfect inside your home after a sewage backup or flood.

What should I do with ruined belongings in Rosedale before the adjuster visits?

Photograph them and keep them until the claim is settled.
