Storm water damage cleanup in Rosedale, NY covers rain and wind damage that enters through the building envelope: missing shingles, failed flashing, broken windows, blown-in doors and wall seams. It is different from rising flood water, which our flood damage cleanup page covers.

Storm loss is time sensitive. Water in insulation and wall cavities lingers long after the clouds clear. Call (516) 845-9582 and we start with extraction, then moisture mapping and drying.

Storm damage emergency: protect people first

After a storm, the building may be unsafe in ways that are not obvious.

Do this first, in this order

  1. Stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings or visible structural damage.
  2. Avoid downed wires and wet electrical equipment. Call your electric utility or 911 for any electrical hazard.
  3. If you can do so safely from inside, place buckets and plastic sheeting to limit further wetting.
  4. Photograph the roof, windows and interior damage before moving anything.
  5. Call (516) 845-9582, then your insurer.

Temporary roof tarping is a roofer's job. Do not climb on a wet or damaged roof.

How storm water gets in

Wind pushes rain sideways under shingles and around windows, where it finds nails, seams and flashing. It tracks down rafters and wall cavities and shows up as stains far from where it entered.

Wet insulation holds water against drywall and framing. Moisture mapping with a meter and thermal camera finds those hidden pockets, and the crew opens walls where readings call for it.

After the storm: what to check on your own

Once it is safe, walk the house with a flashlight. Check ceilings for stains, windows for leaks around the frames, the attic for wet insulation and the basement for seepage. Photograph each problem with a date stamp, even if it looks minor.

Small leaks grow. A stain the size of a coin can mean a wet cavity the size of a door, and a roofer or contractor should look at it soon. Keep receipts for any temporary repairs, since insurers often ask for them.

What to document for your claim

The DFS advises documenting your losses before cleaning anything up, keeping receipts for emergency repairs and holding damaged items until the insurer settles. Good documentation includes dated photos and video, a list of damaged items with approximate value and any notes from your roofer or contractor.
