How to Read a Water Damage Scope of Work in Rosedale, NY

A scope of work is a promise in writing. Reading it closely is how you avoid surprises.

  • Written for Rosedale homeowners
  • 24/7 dispatch
24/7 emergency dispatch(516) 845-9582☎ Call now

A water damage scope of work is the written list of tasks a contractor will perform, in which rooms, with what materials and by what method. It is different from an estimate, which prices the scope, and from an invoice, which bills for it.

For Rosedale, NY homeowners, reading the scope before you sign is the best protection against disputes. We explain scope and price before work starts, and the sections below show what to look for, whether you are working with us or another company. See also structural drying for how monitoring appears in a scope.

The parts of a scope

A good scope has a header with the address and date of loss, a short description of the cause, a room-by-room list of tasks and a section for equipment and monitoring. It should state which materials are being removed and which are being dried in place, with measurements where relevant.

It should also list exclusions. For example, plumbing repairs are not part of restoration work, so a scope should say who is handling the pipe.

Specific language is easier to check.

Emergency scope versus rebuild scope

The first scope usually covers emergency work: extraction, demolition and drying. The second covers rebuilding. They are priced separately because rebuild scope depends on what the drying and demolition reveal.

Do not let a rebuild begin until drying readings meet their goals. Closing walls over damp framing is a common cause of repeat problems.

The standard a scope should follow

A solid scope reflects the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets drying goals and monitoring. Ask where that shows up in the paperwork and who signs off on each reading.

Changes, signatures and what to keep

If hidden damage is found, ask for a written change order that describes the added work and cost before it begins. Keep a signed copy of the scope, the readings log and all receipts.

If your insurer is paying, send them the scope early. The DFS notes that permanent repairs should wait until the insurer has inspected the losses, so ask what to hold off on.

Equipment lines

Look for the number and type of air movers, dehumidifiers and air scrubbers, the rooms they will run in and how long they are expected to stay. Equipment should be placed based on the affected area, and the scope should explain the plan in simple terms.

Ask how the company will decide to add or remove equipment. A daily reading log is the usual answer.

Demolition lines

Demolition lines should say what is removed, how far up a wall and why. Cutting drywall above the wet line is common because wicking carries moisture upward. Ask to see moisture readings at the cut line.

Also ask whether insulation will be removed, how baseboards and trim will be handled and whether they will be saved for reuse.

Rebuild lines

Rebuild lines should list materials and finish levels, such as primer and coats of paint, and should say whether colors will be matched. If flooring is replaced, ask about the product and the transition to existing floors.

If rebuild pricing is not yet available, the scope should say it will follow drying and that a separate scope will be provided.

Photos and readings in the scope

A strong scope refers to photos and moisture readings taken at the start. These show the starting point and let you check progress. Ask for a copy of the initial readings and for updates each day equipment runs.

Key points to remember

Check each of these before you sign.

  • The address, date and cause are correct.
  • Rooms and tasks are listed one by one.
  • Equipment and monitoring are described.
  • Demolition and rebuild are separate.
  • Exclusions are named.
  • Changes require a written change order.

Notes for Rosedale

The NYC Department of Buildings notes that a licensed contractor may do urgent work first and then file an Emergency Work Notification within two business days, and that exempt work must still comply with other laws. Ask your contractor whether your scope includes any filings.

If your property is in Lawrence, the Village Building Department states that a permit must be obtained for all construction including repairs, so confirm the scope covers that. For Cambria Heights homes in the historic districts, add exterior work to the questions you ask. A burst pipe scope usually includes both plumbing and drying contacts.

Common questions

What is the difference between an estimate and a scope?

The scope lists tasks, and the estimate prices them.

Can a scope change after I sign?

Yes, if hidden damage is found, but it should be by written change order.

Should the scope list drying readings?

Yes. It should state that readings will be logged until each material meets its goal.

Who handles permits in Rosedale?

Ask your contractor and the NYC Department of Buildings. Some work is exempt but must still follow other rules.
