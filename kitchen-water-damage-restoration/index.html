Kitchen water damage restoration in Rosedale, NY deals with water under sinks, around dishwashers and refrigerators, behind cabinets and in floors beneath them. Kitchens are packed with water lines and drains, and leaks often run unseen until cabinets swell or floors cup.

This page focuses on the room itself: cabinets, countertops, flooring and the wall behind. If the leak came from a washer or fridge, see appliance leak water damage. Call (516) 845-9582 for an assessment.

Water damage under the kitchen sink

The most common kitchen leak begins under the sink: a loose drain slip nut, a cracked supply line, a worn shutoff or a faucet connection that drips. The cabinet floor soaks first. Particleboard bases swell, edges lift and the cabinet base can crumble within weeks.

Moisture follows pipe penetrations into the wall behind and into the subfloor. A musty smell inside the cabinet or a floor that feels soft near the sink are the early warnings.

What to do before the crew arrives in a kitchen

A wet kitchen mixes water, power and gas, so go in this order.

  • Close the valve under the sink or the main valve.
  • Turn off power to nearby outlets if the cabinet base is wet.
  • Empty cabinets and move items to a dry area.
  • Keep the dishwasher and range off until checked.
  • Photograph the cabinet interiors and the floor.

What crews check in a wet kitchen

Kitchens have layered materials, so the assessment goes slowly and carefully.

  • Cabinet bases and toe-kicks, which hide water for weeks.
  • Flooring edges, especially where it meets cabinets and appliances.
  • The wall behind the sink and range, using a moisture meter and thermal camera.
  • The room below, if there is one, for ceiling staining.
  • Countertop seams and backsplash joints where water can wick.

The result is a written scope showing what can be dried and what must be replaced.

Standards for cabinets and floors

Wet cabinetry, subfloor and wall cavities are dried and judged against the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Ask your company which parts it plans to save and which it plans to remove.

Dishwasher and refrigerator spaces

The space under a dishwasher and behind a refrigerator is where kitchen water hides longest. A dishwasher supply line or drain loop that leaks sends water into the cabinet base and underlayment, and the leak is often small enough that it goes unnoticed until the floor feels spongy near the machine.

Refrigerator ice-maker lines leak slowly behind the unit. Pull the appliance forward and check the floor and wall for staining or soft spots. When we assess, we check beneath and behind appliances because that is where water pools without being seen.

Quick kitchen checks you can do yourself

These checks take a few minutes and do not need tools.

Under the sink

Empty the cabinet, run the tap and drain, and feel the pipes and the base for dampness.

Toe-kicks

Look for swelling, stains or a musty smell along the bottom edge of the cabinets.

Floor near appliances

Press gently on flooring near the dishwasher and refrigerator for soft spots.

Water meter

Turn off all fixtures and watch the meter for movement to rule out a hidden leak.

If anything feels wet or soft, stop using that appliance and call a plumber and us.

Common questions

Can wet cabinets be saved?

Often, if they are solid wood or plywood and dried quickly. Particleboard usually swells and must be replaced.

How do I find a leak under the sink?

Dry the area, run water and check the pipes with a paper towel. Look for moisture on the wall behind too.

Do you replace cabinets?

We handle drying and restoration. Cabinet replacement is scoped after drying, and the contractor must hold the required license.

Is kitchen water damage covered by insurance?

Sudden leaks often are, gradual ones often are not. Ask your insurer.
