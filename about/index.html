Who answers the phone

Rosedale Water Damage Pros is a call-only water damage service for Rosedale, Cambria Heights and Lawrence. When you call, a person asks where the water is coming from, what is wet and whether anyone is in danger. We give you the first safe steps and tell you what happens next.

We connect callers with licensed and insured restoration professionals. They handle the physical work, which is pumping out water, moving contents, opening wet walls, running drying equipment and repairing what was removed.

What happens after you call

Most jobs follow the same order. The source of the water is stopped, standing water is extracted, wet materials that cannot dry are removed and the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Moisture readings are logged until each material reaches its goal. Rebuilding is scoped separately once drying is done.

That order matches the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, which is a published industry document. You can ask any company, including ours, whether its crews work to it. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, which is why we take emergency calls around the clock.

How we talk about price

Scope and price are explained before work starts. We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic, tides and storms change it, and we say so plainly. We give same-day response when available.

We do not use bait pricing, and we do not ask you to sign over insurance benefits on the first call. Read the paperwork, compare it with other written scopes and decide. Ask any company how it measures drying, which standard its crews follow and whether the scope is in writing.

Why Rosedale

Rosedale is a neighborhood of Queens (NYC) on the edge of Jamaica Bay, near the Nassau County line. The National Weather Service New York office records coastal flooding on Hungry Harbor Road, Hook Creek Boulevard and Brookville Boulevard, and Meadowmere saw major residential damage during Hurricane Sandy in 2012.

Those facts shape how a job is planned. Low rooms, basements and crawl spaces are common, tidal water can carry contamination and older finishes behave differently from new ones. Knowing the area helps us ask the right questions on the first call.

What we do not do

Mold work is outside our scope. It follows its own standard, ANSI/IICRC S520, and any health question belongs with a physician. Our part is drying the structure fast, which leaves less moisture for anything to grow on.

We also do not give legal or insurance advice. Our guides explain general rules and point to the agencies that publish them, such as the New York State Department of Financial Services and the NYC Department of Environmental Protection.

How drying is measured

Drying is finished when each material reaches its goal, not when it feels dry. Crews compare moisture readings with an unaffected area of the same material, such as a dry wall in the same room, and they repeat the readings on a schedule. Thermal cameras and humidity logs help them find wet areas that are hidden behind surfaces.

That record is useful to you. It shows when equipment can come out, supports an insurance claim and helps if you sell the home later. Ask for a copy of the readings at the start and at the end of the job.

Questions people ask us most

People ask whether insurance will cover the damage, how long drying takes and whether they can stay in the house. The honest answers are that coverage depends on the cause and your policy, drying often takes several days and staying depends on the size of the loss and whether the water was contaminated.

Call your insurer to ask about coverage, and call us for the physical plan.
