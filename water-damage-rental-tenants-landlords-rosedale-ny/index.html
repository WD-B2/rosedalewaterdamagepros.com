Water Damage in a Rental: Rosedale, NY Tenants and Landlords

Tenants report and protect. Owners repair and dry. Both sides do better with a written record.

  • Written for Rosedale homeowners
  • 24/7 dispatch
24/7 emergency dispatch(516) 845-9582☎ Call now

Water damage in a rental raises questions about who calls whom. In general a tenant should tell the landlord or managing agent right away, take photographs and protect belongings, while the owner arranges repairs and drying. Written notice protects both sides.

This guide offers general information for Rosedale, NY tenants and landlords and is not legal advice. Housing rules are set by the City and the State, so check with the right agency. If the problem started as a clogged drain or sewage backup, report it promptly.

If you are a tenant

Take these steps in order.

  1. Make sure it is safe and shut off water if you can reach the valve.
  2. Photograph and video the damage and your belongings.
  3. Tell the landlord or managing agent in writing, and keep a copy.
  4. Move valuables and electronics away from water.
  5. If a sewer backup is involved, call 311 to report it.
  6. Ask your own insurer about renter's coverage for belongings.
  7. Keep receipts for emergency costs.

Renter's insurance generally covers your belongings, not the building.

What to do first as a tenant or an owner

The first day matters for both sides of a lease.

  • Make the unit safe and stop the water if you can reach the valve.
  • Do not touch electrical items near water.
  • Photograph the damage before moving belongings.
  • Tell the other party in writing.
  • Keep children and pets out of wet rooms.

Standards owners can ask for

Owners can ask a restoration company whether its crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard and request the moisture log. That record helps with insurance and with tenant questions.

If you are a landlord

Respond quickly, document the cause and begin drying. Delay turns a small repair into a bigger one, because wet drywall and subfloor deteriorate fast. A written scope from a restoration company, with photos and moisture readings, helps with insurance and with any tenant questions later.

Keep tenants informed. If an apartment needs to be vacated for drying or repair, put the plan in writing, and consult your attorney or the housing agency about your obligations.

Sewage and contaminated water

DEP asks you to call 311 when raw sewage comes back up into a home, and says it inspects within six hours in many cases. When a private line causes the backup, a Notice to Repair can be issued to the property owner, and the owner is responsible for the sewer service line. DEP also advises thoroughly cleaning and disinfecting after a backup.

Contaminated water changes the plan. Tenants should avoid contact and landlords should use protective cleanup, as outlined in sewage backup cleanup.

Written notice that works

A good notice is short and specific. Give the address and unit, the date you noticed the problem, what you saw and what you have done. Attach photos and state that you are asking for repairs. Send it in a way that leaves a record, and keep a copy.

Follow up if you do not hear back. A second message a day later, again in writing, shows you acted reasonably.

Protecting belongings and staying safe

Lift belongings off wet floors, move electronics away from water and keep children and pets out of affected rooms. Avoid touching wet outlets or switches. If the ceiling is sagging with water, stay out of the room and tell the owner right away.

If you cannot stay in the unit, keep receipts for temporary costs and ask your insurer what your policy may cover.

For owners: working with a restoration company

Give the company access, share any keys or lockbox details and tell tenants when workers are coming. Ask for written updates and drying logs. Share the scope with your insurer and keep tenants informed about timing.

Good communication prevents small problems from turning into disputes.

Moving out temporarily

If drying and repairs make a unit unlivable for a time, talk with the owner about the plan in writing. Ask what is expected, how long it may last and whom to contact. Keep receipts for hotels or meals, since renter's insurance may reimburse some temporary costs depending on the policy.

Key points to remember

Both sides benefit from the same habits.

  • Write down what happened and when.
  • Send notice in writing and keep a copy.
  • Take photos before anything is moved.
  • Tenants insure belongings and owners handle the building.
  • Report sewage to 311 and avoid contact.
  • Start drying quickly, since delays increase the damage.

Notes for Rosedale renters and owners

Rosedale includes many two and three family homes where an upstairs leak lands in a downstairs unit. When that happens, both households should photograph their own side, since water often travels through ceilings and walls to reach a different apartment. Mention the upstairs source in your notice.

Rental units in Cambria Heights and Lawrence work similarly, though Lawrence is in Nassau County, so its village and county agencies handle local questions.

Common questions

Who pays when a pipe bursts in a rental?

The building repair is typically the owner's, and the tenant's belongings fall under the tenant's own insurance. Check your lease.

How should a tenant notify the landlord in Rosedale?

Do it in writing, such as a text or email, and keep a copy with photos.

Can a restoration company work in a rental?

Yes, with the owner's or managing agent's authorization.

What if the water came from the unit above?

Photograph your damage and tell the landlord, who can coordinate both units.
