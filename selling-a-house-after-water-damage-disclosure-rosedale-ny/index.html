Selling a Rosedale, NY Home After Water Damage: What to Disclose

New York's disclosure form now asks direct flood and water questions. Records of proper repair make those answers easier.

  • Written for Rosedale homeowners
  • 24/7 dispatch
24/7 emergency dispatch(516) 845-9582☎ Call now

If your Rosedale, NY home had water damage, a buyer will want to know. New York requires most sellers of one to four family homes to complete a Property Condition Disclosure Statement (PCDS) and deliver it to the buyer, and since March 20, 2024 the old option to pay a $500 credit instead has been eliminated.

This is general information, not legal advice. Talk to your attorney and your real estate agent about what your situation requires. A restoration company can supply documents such as drying logs and a written scope, as described in structural drying, which help show the problem was handled.

What changed in 2024

According to published summaries of the New York law, the credit option is gone and sellers must complete and deliver the form. The form now includes more flood questions. These ask whether the property is in a floodplain or a FEMA flood zone, whether there have been flood insurance claims and whether water has entered the home.

Because the form asks what you know, answers should be accurate and based on your own experience. If a question is unclear, ask your attorney how to answer.

Repairs done right are easier to explain

A buyer or inspector who sees a clean record of a fixed leak, professional drying and a rebuilt wall is likely to feel more at ease than one who finds fresh paint and no paperwork. Keeping documents organized in a folder can shorten negotiations.

Hidden problems are the real risk. If a past leak was never dried, a moisture meter and thermal camera at inspection can reveal it, so treat any doubts honestly before you list. See wall and drywall repair for what a documented fix involves.

Flood versus plumbing water

The form separates ideas that are easy to mix up. A burst pipe, a roof leak and a coastal flood are different events. Flood insurance claims and floodplain status have their own questions. If you are unsure of your property's flood zone, the FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you search an address, so check the FEMA flood map for your address and keep a copy.

Do not guess at a zone. Use the map result and keep it with your sale documents.

Talking with buyers and inspectors

Be ready to explain what happened, when it happened and what was done. Short, factual answers work better than long stories. Offer the documents instead of promising that everything is fine.

If an inspector finds elevated moisture, ask what readings they took and where. A drying log from your contractor can be compared with the inspector's findings.

When to get a pre-listing check

If you are unsure whether a past leak is fully dry, a moisture check before listing can prevent surprises. Finding and fixing a small damp area now is easier than reacting to a buyer's inspection report.

Ask your attorney how to handle any new findings in your disclosure.

Working with your attorney and agent

Your attorney knows how the form works in a sale, and your agent knows how buyers react. Bring them your records early. They can tell you how to describe past events accurately and how to present the repairs without overstating them.

Key points to remember

Before you list, gather these.

  • The completed Property Condition Disclosure Statement.
  • Drying logs and invoices.
  • The plumber's report.
  • Claim paperwork.
  • The FEMA flood map result for your address.
  • Your attorney's advice on any unclear question.

Notes for Rosedale sellers

Some Rosedale blocks lie close to Jamaica Bay, and the tide gauge at Inwood read 10.2 feet on December 23, 2022. That tide reached Hungry Harbor Road south of 148th Avenue.

The City is also completing a $51.8 million sewer and drainage project in Rosedale along Francis Lewis Boulevard, Hook Creek Boulevard and Hungry Harbor Road. The Department of Environmental Protection and Department of Design and Construction announced it in 2024 and expect completion in winter 2026.

Buyers may ask how your block fared in past storms. Sellers in Lawrence or Cambria Heights face similar questions, since the Village of Lawrence publishes its own flood map information and notes that its homeowners policy excludes flood.

Common questions

Do I have to disclose past water damage in New York?

The PCDS asks about water and flood history, and sellers must complete and deliver it. Ask your attorney how to answer.

Can I just pay the $500 credit instead?

That option was eliminated for the form effective March 20, 2024, according to published summaries of the law.

What records help after a leak in Rosedale?

Drying logs, invoices, the plumber report and photos help show the problem was handled.

Where do I check if my address is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and keep a copy of the result.
