A single inch of water across 1,000 square feet is about 623 gallons and weighs more than 5,000 pounds. That is why pumps and extraction units matter more than mops, and why this page covers the first hours of a loss. For the drying that follows, see structural drying and dehumidification.

When standing water is an emergency in your Rosedale home

Water on the floor is an emergency whenever it is deeper than a shallow puddle, keeps rising, or has touched outlets, appliances or a panel. Stay calm and work through the steps below while you wait for the crew.

Do this first, in this order

  1. Stop the source if you can: close the fixture shutoff or the main water valve.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. If water is near the panel or outlets, stay out and call your electric utility or 911 first.
  3. Lift rugs, boxes, electronics and paper onto tables, beds or counters.
  4. Keep children and pets out of the water, especially if it came from a drain, toilet or outdoors.
  5. Call (516) 845-9582 and tell us what is leaking, how deep the water is and which floor it is on.

We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic and the size of the loss vary. We do promise to explain the scope and price before any work starts, and to tell you plainly what we can do the same day.

How extraction works and what comes after

Crews start by finding the lowest point where water gathers. Submersible pumps clear deep water, then wand-style extraction units pull water out of carpet, pad and the top layer of wood or concrete. Hoses run out through a window or door, or to a drain approved for that water.

Extraction is the first phase and never the last. Once visible water is gone, moisture meters and thermal imaging show where it traveled, and air movers and dehumidifiers pull the rest out of the structure. Skipping that step is how a dry-looking floor turns into a swollen baseboard two weeks later.

Industry practice follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. Ask us the same.

Common questions

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Rosedale?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24 hours a day on weekends and holidays. Call, describe the water, and we tell you what we can do the same day without promising an arrival time.

How fast does water need to come out?

Sooner is always better. Materials start absorbing within minutes, and the EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth.

Can I use a wet vacuum from the hardware store?

A shop vacuum helps with a small puddle on a hard floor. It cannot pull water from carpet pad, wall cavities or a subfloor, and it should never be used near wet outlets.

Will my insurance pay for extraction?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, but flood damage is usually excluded without a separate flood policy. Read your policy and ask your insurer before you decide.
