When commercial water damage is an emergency

Any active leak in an occupied building is urgent, especially when it reaches electrical equipment, inventory or neighboring units.

Do this first, in this order

  1. Shut off the water supply to the affected area or the building.
  2. Evacuate people if water is near live equipment or the floor is slick.
  3. Move inventory, records and electronics onto shelves or tables.
  4. Take photos and video before moving anything.
  5. Call (516) 845-9582 and notify your landlord or property manager and your insurer.

For fire sprinkler discharge, contact the fire department or sprinkler contractor first.

What is different about commercial loss

Commercial buildings have more materials to dry: suspended ceilings, carpet tile, commercial drywall, slab floors and shared walls with neighbors. A leak in one suite can reach another in minutes, which makes quick isolation important.

Crews also document the loss for insurance and landlord reporting. A written scope with photos, moisture readings and affected areas helps both owners and tenants discuss responsibility.

Standards for commercial losses

Commercial drying jobs run on the same ANSI/IICRC S500 framework as homes, with heavier paperwork. Property managers and insurers often want moisture logs and a written scope, so ask for them up front.

Documentation that helps owners, tenants and insurers

A commercial loss involves several parties, and clear records prevent disputes. We produce a written scope listing affected rooms, materials, moisture readings and photographs with dates. That document can go to your property manager, adjuster and any neighboring tenants.

Keep your own list of damaged inventory, equipment and fixtures, with purchase dates and approximate values. Save invoices for emergency work and for temporary measures such as relocating stock. These records support a claim and help show what was lost.

Protecting inventory and records

Inventory on the floor wicks water quickly, and paper records and electronics are the most fragile items. Lift stock onto shelving, wrap electronics in plastic and move records to a dry room if the building allows. We can pack out contents for cleaning and drying, and list what was moved.

A short written inventory taken the same day makes the insurance conversation easier. Photograph shelves before and after moving them, and note any items you think are a total loss.
